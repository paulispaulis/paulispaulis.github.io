There is little use in simply working on yourself (though by no means is it a simple thing). You have to use that improved self to improve something around you as well. Start writing your own Odyssey.

This will follow a path of:

1. Notice a problem
2. Notice you care for that problem more than other problems
3. Go fix it

It doesn't need to be some universally agreed upon most efficient problem to fix (I have some beef with people who are into effective altruism), just something your improved self deems a problem, and cares about enough to spend time fixing.

_Sidenote:_

Effective altruism sucks. Doing what your parents tell you to do sucks. Doing what anyone tells you to do sucks. You need the passion and drive of discovering your thing on your own! So tell everyone to back off, do things, try things, and decide what you find purposeful and do that. Don't let anyone tell you shit.

All your life's doing and building can be viewed through the lens of problem –> solution. And it should be! Otherwise that building will be purposeless and what is the point of that.

I enjoy Frankl's view, that this purposeful problem solving is to be found by interacting with the world.

> The true meaning of life is to be discovered in the world rather than within man or his psyche. “The self-transcendence of human existence” – being human always points to something / someone other than oneself, a meaning to fulfil or person to encounter. The more one forgets oneself – serving a cause or loving a person – the more human he is and the more he actualises himself.
> 
> Self-actualisation is possible only as a side-effect of self-transcendence.
> 
> – Viktor Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning

### Practical tips

Always have projects! Recognise and label them as such.

Each to have specified deliverables and a due date, defined in a way where a third party could evaluate whether you've completed the project (this rule helps not to define uselessly loose deliverables).

And treat your projects with passion and respect.

Hormozi said something to the like of:

> Omnipotence is having the least amount of time between thinking it and it being so.

So once you've decided on your next priority project – do it!

I am currently in a wardrobe full of dancers in between the dress rehearsal and live show. I have 1.5 hours. I can sit on my phone. I can chat. Or I can get an hour of work done. (Not saying socialising is bad and you always have to be productive, but we had an evening together yesterday, we've had rehearsals and dress times and make-up to chat, I can take an hour here and there to work).

**Always be aware of your priority project (SINGULAR).**

The world is not ideal. You sometimes can't focus on just a single project for weeks at a time. Even so, pick one to define as your priority, and try to reserve as long a block of time as you can for a single project.

This longer focus on the same project allows your subconscious to get involved (Rian Doris had a good video about this, look up "Rian Doris rumination"). Instead of your brain jumping to the stickiest thought when it has a free moment (I should've said this, and how dare she do that) you can actually start to have ideas relating to your priority project while in the shower, on a walk, talking to people.

So leave long blocks of time (3 days or more ideally; start where you can) for the same project, so that your whole life can give you inspiration on your priority tasks.
