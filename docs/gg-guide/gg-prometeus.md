The final chapter on this guide, once your head is clear, you've built yourself up, and you've started building, now it is time to share what you've figured out, discovered, what worked and what didn't.

You don't have to wait until you are a master to teach. You likely already have something in your head that would be beneficial for someone to hear.

It is your responsibility to find it within yourself (likely by trial and feedback), share it with the world in a way that they can understand, and market it such that it reaches the right audience (most easily done through authentic content).

This is what I am doing here.

Started my page, newsletter, youtube, and now creating a guide (as a free lead magnet first, while developing). Will it be perfect? Likely not, but I'll learn a ton, and when I get actual feedback I'll learn even more.

Always problem first.

Give direction to your creations, not have them be aimless preachings. Try to get as specific as you can (rich coming from a guide to life... but this is the first iteration, and if I feel it is too loose I'll trim it).

---

Take apprentices! Can be informal. I've learnt the most from mentors, helping them do what they do, observing, helping; first a little, eventually a lot. So I try to pass it along. Currently I do it on a small scale, some younger dancers and creatives, who I know, who know me, who trust me, who likely want to somewhat grow up as me. So I try to help out how I can.

Help your friends! Especially the close ones. See where you have knowledge that they would benefit from. If you act in line with your teachings they might even approach you themselves; that would be best, as forcing opinions upon people is difficult to do well and even more difficult for them to be well received.

And don't shy away from teaching your mentors and elders. This is a challenging proposition, as you have to gain their trust and respect, and be sparse with what you try and tell them. Too much and it won't be received. But small, confident pieces, based in knowledge they know and have experienced themselves; where they've even asked! But remember their life experience, take into account the differing perspectives, have no judgement in your tone.

---

That is the end of my first attempt at packaging my experience and what has helped me so far in getting to a life that is pretty awesome already, and feels like it has barely even started.
