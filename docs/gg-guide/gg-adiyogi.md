Clarity of thought, clarity of action.

People too often swim through life, unaware, pulled around by the loudest voices and stickiest thoughts. Goals set by others, procrastinating until the anxiety becomes unbearable, spending an unjustifiable amount of time on the whims of others or vices you'd be too ashamed to even write out fully in your own most private of journals.

Your inner voice drowned out and your mind never given the opportunity to process life and how to respond to it; instead every single waking moment is filled with useless noise, chatting, "news", aimless scrolling, unfulfilled desires and procrastinated tasks. Your mind is overflowing with uselessness and given no opportunity to think and feel and settle. In this chaos you are pulled around by the most shameless puller.

Imagine a life where instead of mindless busyness, every action is clear, deliberate, purposeful.

You don't hide from life, you make love with it.

Control attention, control action.

This is the life I refer to as "Adiyogi", named after the first yogi who was always either in ecstatic dance or absolute stillness. A figure of immense wisdom, tranquility, and clarity. A symbol of self-realisation and enlightenment.

Reaching a level of awareness and understanding where your actions naturally align with your purpose.

The dancer, the endless traveller, the lover, and the somewhat reluctant teacher.

Adiyogi has been a personal goal of mine for years, ever since I first heard of him. I resonated with his passion for dance and admired how it was combined with this deep confidence. His crystal clarity and need for nothing, yet passionate for the world and love. Finally his calmness to rest fully in between his actions (a personal struggle of mine, I always feel the need to move and do something, even when dead tired).

I've tried various forms of meditation and yoga and breathwork. I've journaled braindumps and gratitudes and detailed projects. I've abstained from various vices for varied lengths of time. And I've also tried things some would call vices.

Most practices did not stick. At least not in the form they were presented. But taking bits and pieces of each I've found a practice that works for me! Try it whole, to find what parts work for you.

What you need is some combination of practices that strain your body, then fix the kinks that you got while straining. That strain your mind, then fix the kinks that you got while straining. The kink fixing needs to be aware and intelligent, so the straining is processed and learnt from, not wiped completely.

One practice that we all take part in that follows this cycle – wakefulness and sleep. You are awake and strain and do, then sleep fixes and processes what you went through so you are better prepared for the next day.

All these other practices that we try are covering parts of this same need for strain and then process and fix the kinks.

Body strain and kink-fix I will cover in the Demigod section. Some general straining will be covered in the Odyssey section. Some general kink-fixing and processing will be covered right here.

I'll organise them by problem questions.

### _Life doesn't spark joy. Even the parts that I used to enjoy have lost their shine._

> Need for rest and fixing the kinks. Give yourself mental room to process.

The most effective method I've found is to temporarily abstain from highly dopaminergic activities. I only had to do a hard dopamine detox once (that was three and a half years ago) and the problem of activities lacking joy hasn't returned.

I've returned to forms of dopamine deprivation since then, but for other purposes (currently reduce mindless youtube intake to have extra time and clarity for the high amount of projects that are happening at the same time).

For the problem of lack of joy (as Huberman defines addiction, a narrowing of what brings pleasure), I followed a Anna Lembke described dopamine detox once, for a month.

My format then was 1 month of:

- no youtube
- no tv series or movies
- no social media
- no porn
- no podcasts or audiobooks
- no alcohol
- no weed

Add to that whatever else is relevant. I allowed myself to read books as that wasn't highly dopaminergic for me, but if you do all-nighters of crime flicks, toss that shit out (for a month).

Find an accountability buddy! A good friend of yours that you force yourself to check in regularly with (and who checks in on you if you miss out). These can also be found online, where you find a person and you will each be the other's accountability bud. Be honest! Even if you slip up for a bit, that's okay – you got the first rep in. Now get back to it.

What to expect in this month?

The first two weeks felt horribly. In the evenings when I was at home I simply lay on the couch in weird despair. Nothing bad had happened, but it felt like my innards were falling out. I talked to my brother a lot. Worked in a cafe to have some social ambience. Went to my dance classes. (was much too enthusiastic in group chats... if it is too big a problem cut that out as well)

The next two weeks got better. After the full month... Nothing has ever really been the same again.

I've returned to taking occasional part in the highly dopaminergic activities, but they haven't returned in any long term over-consuming form.

I love my life. The things I get to do. The only "problem" is that there are too many fun things to do and I sometimes have to choose which to put off for a while.

### _I am tired and confused. Everything is so fast and noisy and overwhelming._

Spend a whole day walking in nature.

My favourite is the forest right by the sea. (No phone or music! Walk, think, process. Better not to even chat. If you've brought someone, walk with them in silence.)

Then spend the whole following day sleeping.

I'm talking chill wake up, chat, walk, eat, fall asleep at 6pm, wake at midnight, check what thoughts clamour in your head, do your meditative practice of choice for 30 minutes, then fall back asleep until 10am.

This will already greatly improve everything. But for even more bang for the buck. During this whole reset practice, occasionally (a few times over the whole two days) redirect your attention to your life, what you are doing, what you want to be doing.

Then find some moments (after the walk) to write down some version of a plan. What is happening, in what order will you tackle it. How will you get around to making your life what you want it to be?

Face the discomfort. Plot a path.

After this two day reset, you don't want to return to how it was, so you need a regular meditative practice.

I don't enjoy meditating.

At least not the usual form. The sitting doesn't feel comfortable, I can't do it when I'm sleepy. I've had pleasurable experiences, but never had success importing it into my daily life.

But I found a practice that did import! It can be done when sleepy. The posture while aligning and demanding, doesn't frustrate me as much. And it has a bodily component that I enjoy more than the usual yogic meditative poses. This I cover in the Demigod section, as it was introduced to me as a method of fixing bodily kinks, but since it is 30 minutes of not moving, it naturally became a nice meditative practice.

So try it! Do it daily. First thing in the morning, or any time during the day, or when you are going to bed. As you've put your alarm on just do the pose for 30 minutes, it will prime you for sleep, do not care that it removes 30 minutes from your sleep time, it will improve the sleep and life quality enough that it is worth it!

That is it. Two day reset, regular meditative practice. Enjoy life until you feel the need for another greater adventure.

### _I approach life tensely. There is constant tightness in the front of my body (chest, heart, solar plexus, stomach). I cannot seem to open and connect to the world around me._

Here I borrow a method from David Deida. It is very simple; he describes versions of it in all of his books that I've read (building on top of it with other more fine tuned practices, but this is the base).

Inhaling down the front of your body. Exhaling up the spine.

My first transformative experience with it went like this. I went to the park on a sunny day, had one of Deida's books with me. I sunbathed as I read the book. There were other people sunbathing as well; some asleep, or on their phones, or reading. The book got to the section on breath. I did the exercises as I read it, the book already having placed me in a calm, open mood. And I could feel the other people in the park opening as I breathed. Their tension fell, their eyes started to wander. This little section of the park relaxed and grew more connected.

Here's a quick guide on how to try it (afterwards you can do it whenever wherever in parallel to your life):

1. Position: Start by sitting comfortably in a chair or on the floor with your back straight. Close your eyes and relax your body. **TRY THIS NOW!**
2. Breathing: Begin to focus on your breath. Inhale deeply through your nose, visualising the breath moving down the front of your body. Feel the breath filling your chest, abdomen, and then lower down to your perineum.
3. Exhale: As you exhale, visualise the breath moving up your spine, from the perineum to the top of your head. You can do this through your nose or mouth, whichever feels most comfortable.
4. Tensing the Perineum: Optionally, as you exhale, you can gently tense your perineum (the area between your genitals and your anus; as if you were trying not to pee). This can help you to feel more grounded and connected to your body.
5. Repeat: Continue this cycle of inhaling down the front of your body and exhaling up your spine for several minutes. As you do so, try to keep your mind focused on the movement of the breath.
6. Finish: To finish the exercise, simply allow your breath to return to its normal rhythm and slowly open your eyes. Take a moment to notice how you feel. You may feel more relaxed, aware, and present.

Remember, this is a practice, and it may take some time to get used to the visualisation and the optional tensing of the perineum.

This is a good small practice that can be done whenever. While working, walking, spending time with friends. To open up.

### Other worthy mentions:

**Nature. Adventure. Taking walks.** The world flowing past you (Huberman optic flow shoutout). This can be a small daily activity or a full day adventure you take weekly! I am still working on a systematic integration of this in my life, but I've gotten a motorcycle license for adventures (and the bike itself... Kawasaki Versys 650 2011, I like it! It is winter now though so it is resting) and I do regular walks. But I am in the process of organising more regular greater adventures. Will report how that is going in due time (to keep up [follow my newsletter](https://from.paulis.id.lv/) or [subscribe to me on youtube](https://youtube.com/@paulisbarzdins?si=zH9SbfwfGzMNAZx6)).

**Amanita Muscaria.** Or Fly Agaric. You can read up online on it, it is legal in most countries (except New Zealand i think?) and can often even be picked on your own. This won't yet be a comprehensive guide, but I feel it has played a role in my path towards Adiyogi (for example I made amanita tea during the life changing dopamine detox 3.5 years ago that I described above. I took it one week into the detox. It helped a lot! Subjectively of course).

### RECAP:

A list of practices:

- Dopamine Detox
- lying on stomach pose (30 daily minutes of aligned pose and body scan, awareness)
- David Deida breathing (inhale down the front, exhale up the spine)
- nature, walks, adventure
- Amanita Muscaria

All of these I am actively practicing. All of these were the most effective for me. Allowing you to make love with life instead of merely enduring it.
