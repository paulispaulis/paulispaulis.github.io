The Body of the Gods

Consider the ancient figures of the gods - Hercules, Apollo, Artemis. The sculpted forms etched on ancient friezes and paintings, imbued with divinity and reflecting strength, fluidity, and competence. Now visualise yourself as such a deity – a living pantheon of power. This is our agenda.

The common perspective is that a situation like yours - so far removed from physical activity due to the hustle of modern life - is acceptable. It is a perspective that adheres to the minimum – performing some physical activity, just enough to avoid discomfort. (I have a colleague that says he has checked research and so does 20 minutes of stationary bike a day. And that is it. No progressive overload, no heart rate minimums, nothing. It was a good first step, but he is so overdue for the next one. So what he is doing is the bare minimum so that he can, in his mind, check off the box - health.) But this perspective (of bare minimum) is wrong and harmful.

The body is a tool and, like any tool, requires care and maintenance. Undervalued, it decays. It becomes weak, incapable, and unattractive. The strength to perform everyday tasks diminishes. Aesthetics succumb to the entropy of neglect and disuse. The diminished physical capability is the enemy we wage war against.

Imagine, then, the power and allure of a demigod.

A transformation through focused purpose. The balance between strength, skill, and attractiveness created through a directed mind and a dedicated regimen.

My personal blend is dance (contemporary, ballet, folk, etc), bodybuilding, acrobatics, martial arts, and every sport I can get my hands on to try (not all at the same time all the time!). My personal result is strength, skill, aesthetics, and enough health (I do overstrain occasionally for specific performances).

<img src="https://public-files.gumroad.com/unvzwrunitjh9fgr4zpvprp0nw2d" alt="Description of the image" style="width: 50%; height: auto;">

Dance teaches me control, rhythm, and flexibility. Bodybuilding allows me to target specific lacking bits of the physique for strength and aesthetics. The combination of these two creates a perfect balance in the body, like the strength and grace of a demigod of old.

The result? A body capable and sturdy, aesthetically striking, a body embodying the essence of a demigod. A change that radiates outward, invigorating every facet of life. It's a personal testament to human possibility.

And what is even more fun? I am good at almost any activity on the first go! I can make the most out of all the opportunities life throws my way. Whether it be getting more out of a single lesson of acrobatics, or a single day on a wake board or jet ski or snowboarding (when skiing is usually my thing).

Fuelled by the promise of divine transformation, consider your training as a game, a journey of self-discovery and relentless improvement. The first level in this game is gaining resistance to entropy - becoming better today than you were yesterday.

Divine attributes aren't received overnight. They are earned with consistency and perseverance. Protein feeds the growth. Sweat baptises the effort. Increasing challenges, matched with increasing strength and skill, culminate in a spectacle of improvement, a hierarchy of achievements that propels you towards your goal.

So commence the ascendance towards divinity, my friend. Extol the virtues of discipline, consistency, and hard work. Embrace the dance, the martial endeavours, and nutrition. Remember, this isn't just about curating a body. It's carving out your place among the gods.

### Practical tips:

Passion will be the best long term motivator. But it doesn't come for free!

Currently i am psychopathic with my movement; i dance most days for hours at rehearsals, on the off days i murder my muscles at the gym, every day i walk for the bare minimum of an hour, and i take every opportunity to go hiking skiing ice skating (it is winter now), swimming bike riding and spiking (spike-ball for the win).

I didn't start off passionate for dance. Or the gym. Or weighted stretching (still in the process for this actually).

You try things, give each a couple of sessions, see that you don’t hate it, maybe even like something about it. Then you put the work in. The level of love and enjoyment i have for dance exploded … 8 years after starting. And is still growing (13 years in).

I started going to the gym only in university, my first "coach" was a roommate who mainly did cable workouts and some recovery exercises for his injuries. But I knew I wanted to get stronger (so I can literally pick up girls in dance above my head with ease – which I have reached! pics below) and bigger (straight aesthetics) so the gym was a must. I went with friends, tried each of their routines, I watched every youtube fitness video, I looked at what the gym goals people in my gym were doing and copied them. Years went by and I slowly found my own groove, what I liked, what I needed, what worked. This guide can then be one of the inputs you take, for finding your own groove.

### So how do you start?

It is the same process, rinse and repeat:

1. Have a tingling to what you want
2. Try activities that might align
3. When something somewhat doesn't suck, stick out the struggle phase
4. The dopamine of learning and improving will kick in (can be in as little as a couple of weeks if done often enough)

For me it was:

1. I need some sport as a kid (because I left basketball)
2. Went along with my brother to dance school tryouts
3. Stuck around for a couple of sessions, suddenly we are preparing for a concert
4. 2 months in was my first performance (large dance installation, pic below), I was hooked

<img src="https://public-files.gumroad.com/5gl899y83mvs21jkjjqh2mzprqgc" alt="Description of the image" style="width: 80%; height: auto;">

That's a tiny me! I was looking for any generic pic from the performance, and stumbled upon an article with a tiny me on the front of it. I don't care that it is blurry, that is a tiny happy me dancing there.

Be wary of skipping steps.

I had a situation where I got a new friend who was a runner, had taken a break for an injury and was getting back into it as we met. So we decided I'd tag along. I tried it, somewhat liked it, so we went on some more runs every now and then. But I never got into a groove with it. I didn't stick it through even though I somewhat liked it. Why?

Notice that I skipped step 1 – have a tingling for what you want. I didn't need a new sport. Dance covered my muscle endurance volume, my cardio was good enough, and I needed all the recovery I could get to grow at all in the gym.

You don't need mindless busyness, for love of bustle is not industry, it is only the restlessness of a hunted mind (Seneca).

Build your set of activities to align with your vision for your life (but that vision better include finding out the beauty your body is capable of for...

> No man has the right to be an amateur in the matter of physical training. It is a shame for a man to grow old without seeing the beauty and strength of which his body is capable.

> – Socrates

### What should you start with?

**For an incredible foundation for all other ventures – DANCE.**

Dance gives an incredible base for everything else; knowing how to move your body translates to literally any activity.

Look for local classes. Ballet will give you great strength (though classes will be intimidating if you are a beginner). Bachata is a fun partner dance! Contemporary classes are good for starting to move, I like if they have handstands and their variations, entering and exiting the floor, jumps; with those the contemporary class will cover most of the body's physical requirements.

**For the option with the least friction – GYM.**

Lower cost alternative – calisthenics, youtube is full of videos.

You can sign up and go today, you don't have to talk to anyone, and you can start by following any routine on the internet (==or try mine, which I have yet to write :D placeholder, you are witnessing me trying to create a connected web of content==) and in time you'll learn what you do and don't enjoy and fine-tune it.

Don't know what program to take? Start with bodybuilding. You'll start moving, you'll like how you look, and in time you'll get exposed to other options and maybe something will stand out and you'll try it then. You could start with the big three lifts, that will also get your dopamine rushing as the numbers increase, but I find the higher risk of injury to not be worth it; I rather save my joints to be messed up in dance.

**For an easier, less intimidating start – WALK.**

Start taking walks. I like not using my phone, not listening to anything. Give the brain the option to process life. And if you walk fast enough, or up a hill, or run, it can be a start on your fitness journey (with the added benefit of processing life).

### Fixing the kinks you got while straining

I'll start with a quick list of goodies:

- cold exposure
- heat exposure
- massage

My versions of these?

- I finish all my morning showers with the coldest water; my post-gym showers as well. That is what I do every day. Then whenever I can I swim in open water (all year long), roll around in snow, knock out an "āliņğis" (ice hole) to go swim in it.
- Sauna, I have one in my gym. I am lucky enough that some friends have one. Again, whenever the opportunity presents itself – I take it. Regularly at the gym, then psycho mode with friends.
- Massage gun for a quick massage whenever you need it. If you can get another person to do it, even better (can decide if you reward them "naudā, graudā, vai baudā").

There is another practice I take part in, with an unlikely origin story.

I first encountered it when visiting a friend of mine when we were in middle school. I was staying overnight and before bed he suddenly laid down on the floor on his stomach in a specific pose.

I was very intrigued and also wanted to try it.

It was his mom who had prescribed the position to him; at first she was reluctant to show me how to do it (as I was a kid, and not hers) but I convinced her to let me try.

She is a swimming teacher and coach currently writing her PhD on primitive reflexes (the reflexes that should flush out of your brain as you leave the womb but they overstay their welcome and leave maladapted patterns in your body).

There is a specific sequence of how to lay down, then how to hold your body (not relaxed, but aligned; as the relaxed position of a maladapted body isn't optimal). I CANNOT DESCRIBE IT HERE, but if you want a guide, do [LEAVE A COMMENT ON MY LATEST VIDEO](https://youtube.com/@paulisbarzdins?si=zH9SbfwfGzMNAZx6) so I know there is interest and I'll try to make a video showing how to do it. Though it might require some feedback and coaching (I have gone back to the friend's mom multiple times, we have another appointment scheduled where she will show me other poses that can be attached to the first) I'll figure something out.

In the mean time, what I think brought the benefits is a combination of an aligning pose for the body, a half-hour with no noisy input, the body scan you do during it. So while you wait for my guide you can do a favourite meditation, or lay on the ground for a body scan (though you will likely fall asleep, but a 30 minute nap isn't the worst thing you can do to yourself!).
